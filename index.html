Preprocessing pipelines under distribution shift

For my M.S. thesis at Purdue, I worked on a question that connects my research and engineering interests: when the data changes, how can we choose a suitable preprocessing pipeline without evaluating the entire search space again?

My approach reuses pipeline evaluations from historical data regions. A shared representation of feature distributions, missingness, outliers, and category rarity supports both historical segmentation and retrieval. Regional pipelines handle different parts of the incoming data, with a global pipeline as a fallback.

I evaluated the method on Bank Marketing and Diabetes Readmission. For the 96-candidate Diabetes search space, the incoming search evaluates three candidates: 96.9% fewer candidate evaluations, excluding historical construction and final refits. I successfully defended this work under Dr. Romila Pradhan.

M.S. thesis: Historical Segmentation for Budgeted Preprocessing Pipeline Selection under Distribution Shift.

Fairness through task-vector arithmetic

As a Graduate Research Assistant in Purdue's Responsible Data Science Lab from August 2024 to May 2025, I worked with Dr. Romila Pradhan on using task-vector arithmetic to reduce bias in machine learning models.

Our CERIAS poster received second place in the 2025 poster competition. I also presented this work at Purdue AARC Applied AI Day.

  • Pote, O. and Pradhan, R. “Unlearning Machine Learning Bias using Task Vector Arithmetic.” 26th CERIAS Annual Cybersecurity Symposium, Purdue University, 2025. Second Place, Poster Competition.
  • Pote, O. (Advisor: Dr. Romila Pradhan). “Resolving Machine Learning Bias using Task Vector Arithmetic.” Purdue AARC Applied AI Day, 2025.

During the assistantship, I also explored large model management and model lakes, studying metadata quality, provenance, versioning, and model discovery. This was exploratory work, separate from my M.S. thesis.

Counterfactual explanations for time series

At IIT Madras, I worked as a Project Scientist in the DART / iACE Labs under Dr. Rajagopalan Srinivasan from October 2023 to June 2024.

I developed model-agnostic counterfactual explanations for multivariate time-series classification, focusing on process fault diagnosis and recovery. This work brought my chemical engineering background together with my interest in understanding machine learning decisions.

Peer-reviewed short paper: Pote, O., Majji, D., Bhakte, A., Srinivasan, B., and Srinivasan, R. “Timeseries-based Counterfactual Explanations for Process Fault Diagnosis and Recovery.” 35th European Symposium on Computer Aided Process Engineering (ESCAPE), 2025. Book of Short Papers, pp. 235–236.
