Preprocessing pipelines under distribution shift

For my M.S. thesis, I looked at what happens when the data changes and the preprocessing pipeline needs to change with it. Trying every possible configuration again gets expensive. I wanted to see how much we could reuse from past experiments.

The idea is to split historical data into regions, keep track of which pipelines worked there, and use that history to narrow down the search for new data. I compare things like distributions, missing values, outliers, and rare categories. Different parts of the new data can then use different pipelines, with a global one to fall back on.

I tested this on Bank Marketing and Diabetes Readmission. For Diabetes, the incoming search tries just three candidates from a space of 96 (96.9% fewer candidate evaluations, excluding the historical work and final refits). I worked on this with Dr. Romila Pradhan, and recently defended it.

M.S. thesis: Historical Segmentation for Budgeted Preprocessing Pipeline Selection under Distribution Shift.

Fairness through task-vector arithmetic

During my research assistantship at Purdue's Responsible Data Science Lab in 2024–2025, I worked with Dr. Romila Pradhan on task-vector arithmetic: using differences between model weights to try to reduce bias in machine learning models.

I presented this work at CERIAS and Purdue AARC Applied AI Day in 2025. Our CERIAS poster even won second place!

  • Pote, O. and Pradhan, R. “Unlearning Machine Learning Bias using Task Vector Arithmetic.” 26th CERIAS Annual Cybersecurity Symposium, Purdue University, 2025. Second Place, Poster Competition.
  • Pote, O. (Advisor: Dr. Romila Pradhan). “Resolving Machine Learning Bias using Task Vector Arithmetic.” Purdue AARC Applied AI Day, 2025.

I also spent some time exploring large model management and model lakes: how we keep track of models, where they came from, their different versions, and how to find the right one. That was exploratory work, separate from my thesis.

Counterfactual explanations for time series

Before Purdue, I worked with Dr. Rajagopalan Srinivasan in the DART / iACE Labs at IIT Madras as a Project Scientist (October 2023–June 2024).

I worked on counterfactual explanations for multivariate time series: figuring out what would need to change in a signal for a model to make a different prediction. The focus was on process fault diagnosis and recovery, so my chemical engineering background finally got to meet my interest in machine learning. This work led to a short paper at ESCAPE 2025.

Peer-reviewed short paper: Pote, O., Majji, D., Bhakte, A., Srinivasan, B., and Srinivasan, R. “Timeseries-based Counterfactual Explanations for Process Fault Diagnosis and Recovery.” 35th European Symposium on Computer Aided Process Engineering (ESCAPE), 2025. Book of Short Papers, pp. 235–236.

POTATO

During the three years I worked at Capillary, my biggest project was POTATO (Over-The-Air Transactions Orchestrator), an in-house payment orchestration service. It connected payment providers, fuel stations, and third-party services to make cashless fuel purchases work.

Built with Camunda, Spring Boot, MySQL, MongoDB, and RabbitMQ, the system supported more than 10,000 transactions per minute. I also worked on the CI/CD pipelines and monitoring.

ReOn

Before POTATO, I worked as a Big Data Engineer on ReOn, short for Relational Ontologies. It's Capillary's data warehousing and ETL system, powering its CRM and business intelligence workflows. The Spark-based system processed more than 6 billion new records every day.

I worked on the ETL workflows, REST APIs, and Spark queries running on AWS EMR. As an intern, I also built tools to control and monitor those workflows, whose execution graphs were stored in Neo4j.
